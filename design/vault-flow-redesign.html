Sealing a secret should be three decisions, not eleven

The audited vault asks the user to confront secret number, scramble code, lock recipe and box style as form fields before they can act — four cryptographic internals presented as choices, each with a 15 px “what is this?” button. None of them are decisions a person can meaningfully make: three are auto-generated and the fourth cannot be changed.

Meanwhile the two moments that genuinely decide whether this works — send the phrase on a different channel and lose the phrase and it is gone forever — were plain paragraphs below the fold.

The redesign inverts that. Generated values move out of the form and into a receipt shown after sealing, where they are evidence rather than input. The handoff and the irreversibility get the layout weight instead.

What changed, and why


      
Eleven fields became three. Salt, nonce, KDF profile and format version are no longer inputs. They are generated, then reported in a receipt after the fact. A user cannot make a better choice than the default for any of them, so asking was pure cognitive load.


      
The phrase is generated by default, not blank. The audited page started empty and disabled the button until the phrase was strong enough — so the first thing a new user met was a refusal. Now a strong phrase is already there and the meter explains what it is worth. (The generator itself was the finding: 180 words all starting a–e, ~36.6 bits. It now draws on 7,776 words for ~58 bits.)


      
The handoff got the layout. “Send the file and the phrase through different channels” was a sentence under the fold. It is now a two-column diagram at the moment it becomes actionable — right after sealing, which is the only time the user can still act on it.


      
Clearing is a control, not a page reload. The audit found decrypted plaintext stayed rendered on screen after a later failed unlock, with no way to remove it. Every screen that can show plaintext now has an explicit destructive-styled Clear.


      
Every target is at least 44 px. Seventeen controls measured under the 24 px WCAG 2.2 minimum, the worst being the 15 px buttons that explained the cryptography. The explanations are now inline text at the step that needs them, so there is nothing tiny left to tap.
